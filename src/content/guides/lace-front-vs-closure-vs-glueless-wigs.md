Shopping for a wig online comes with its own vocabulary. Lace front, frontal, 4x4 closure, 13x4, HD lace, glueless. Product pages use these words as if everyone already knows them.

This guide explains each term in plain language, how each looks on deeper skin tones, and how to choose.

## The short answer

- **Closure wig:** a small square of lace at the front, usually 4x4 or 5x5 inches. Easy to wear, lower upkeep, limited parting.
- **Frontal wig (often sold as a "lace front"):** lace across the whole hairline, ear to ear, often 13x4 or 13x6. Part it anywhere and pull it back, but expect more upkeep.
- **Glueless wig:** any wig designed to stay put without adhesive, using combs, clips or an adjustable band. Glueless wigs can still have a closure or a frontal.

So "glueless" is not a third type of lace. It describes how the wig is held on, not how much lace it has.

## What is a closure wig?

A closure is a small patch of lace at the front and centre of the wig. Hair is tied into the lace, so the parting looks like it grows from your scalp.

The most common sizes are 4x4 and 5x5 inches. A 5x5 gives you a little more room to move your parting. Mayvenn's guide describes a closure as "a small patch of lace, 4x4 or 5x5 inches, that sits at the front of an install".

**What a closure does well:**

- It is quicker to put on and needs less maintenance.
- It usually costs less than a frontal of the same hair.

**Where it is limited:**

- You get two or three parting options, such as middle or either side.
- You cannot slick it all the way back into a high ponytail, because the lace stops a few inches back and the wefts begin behind it.

## What is a frontal or lace front wig?

A frontal covers the whole hairline from ear to ear and runs a few inches back. The usual size is 13x4, with 13x6 for a deeper parting space. Retailers often call these "lace front wigs".

You can part it anywhere and wear it pulled back, half up or in a ponytail.

**What a frontal does well:**

- The most styling freedom.
- The most natural-looking hairline when it is well laid.

**What it asks of you:**

- More time to install and more care to keep it looking fresh.
- A higher price than a closure in the same hair.
- More lace to blend, which matters for colour matching (more on that below).

## What does "glueless" actually mean?

A glueless wig is made to stay in place without glue or tape. Un-ruly's glueless guide describes it as "a full or partial-coverage wig that stays in place without adhesive", held by "adjustable elastic bands, combs, clips, or even silicone strips for grip".

Many glueless wigs have a closure or a frontal. Many also come with pre-cut lace and pre-plucked hairlines, so there is less to do before you wear them.

**Why people choose glueless:**

- You can put it on and take it off yourself, on the same day.
- No adhesive on your skin or near your edges.
- Easy to swap styles through the week.

**The trade-offs:**

- A glueless unit can shift a little during the day, especially if your head falls between standard cap sizes.
- An elastic band that is too tight can cause tension or headaches, so adjust it to snug, not tight.
- Very sleek, high styles can take practice without adhesive.

## Choosing lace for deeper skin tones

Lace colour matters as much as lace size, and it matters most on a frontal, where there is more of it on show.

- **Transparent lace** is a clear, lightly tinted standard lace. Luvme Hair's guide says it blends most easily on lighter to medium skin, and that "on medium-deep to deep complexions, transparent lace can appear slightly ashy/gray unless you tint it."
- **HD lace** is thinner and more sheer. The same guide says it can work across skin tones, but on deeper complexions "installation and tint choice matter". It is also more delicate and can tear if handled roughly.

What this means in practice: if you have a deep skin tone, plan to tint the lace (or buy a lace shade made for deeper tones) whichever type you choose. And handle HD lace gently when you put it on and take it off.

## Which one should you choose?

Ask yourself three questions.

**1. How do you want to wear it?** If you love ponytails, slicked-back styles or changing your parting, a frontal gives you the most freedom. If you mostly wear your hair down with one parting, a closure covers it.

**2. How much time do you want to spend?** If you want something you can put on in minutes before work, look at glueless closure wigs. If you enjoy the process of laying a hairline, or have a stylist who does it, a frontal rewards the effort.

**3. How is your hairline right now?** If your edges need a rest, a glueless unit with a well-fitted band is the gentler place to start. If you have any concerns about thinning, speak to a dermatologist first.

For a first wig bought online, a glueless closure wig is a gentle, lower-effort place to start.
